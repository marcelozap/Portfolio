## LinkedIn Post

I Had a Dream.

Not that kind of dream.

I literally had a dream last night.

It was vivid, strange, and emotional - and like most dreams, I could already feel pieces of it disappearing when I woke up.

So I opened ChatGPT and started talking.

I described what I remembered.

One detail led to another.

The setting.

The people.

What happened.

How it felt.

As I kept talking, I realized I was not really using AI to interpret my dream.

I was using it to remember.

It also made me wonder what dreams reveal about what the mind is already processing.

If every part of me is moving toward a different goal, no amount of effort feels clean. The closer my attention, habits, and energy move in the same direction, the more momentum I can create.

By the end of the conversation, something that probably would have disappeared from my memory within a few hours had become a structured record I could save.

And then I had another thought:

Why not build this?

An AI dream journal.

Nothing huge. No giant startup plan. Just a small tool solving a problem I experienced that morning.

The workflow could be incredibly simple:

Dream -> Conversation -> Structured Entry -> Searchable Memory

And this connects directly to what I wrote yesterday:

Choose a real problem.
Build something small.
Document what you learn.

I think one of the biggest mistakes we make with AI is starting with the technology.

What can AI do?

Maybe the better question is:

What did I just do manually that a computer could help me do better?

Pay attention to your own life.

The problems are already there.

Sometimes the next thing worth building is not hidden in a market report or a brainstorming session.

Sometimes you just wake up from it.

Day 2 of documenting what I learn building with AI.
