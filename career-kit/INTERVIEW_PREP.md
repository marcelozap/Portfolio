# Marcelo Interview Prep

Use this to prepare for software engineer, QA automation, SDET, and workflow/internal-tools interviews.

## Core message

You are a software engineer with 4 years of experience working on operational systems that matter. Your advantage is not hype. Your advantage is reliability, automation, support ownership, and the ability to work in messy real-world environments.

## 60-second intro

Hi, I’m Marcelo Zapata. I’m a software engineer with 4 years of experience at Publix working across warehouse systems, QA automation, analytics, workflow tooling, and regulated operational software. A lot of my work has involved improving reliability, modernizing older systems, building repeatable automation, and supporting software that people actually depend on day to day. I’ve also taken on more responsibility mentoring newer teammates and helping standardize testing and delivery habits. Outside of work, I build products like Rally and GATEKPT, which has helped me keep growing on the product and engineering side too. Right now I’m looking for a remote or hybrid role with more ownership and room to grow across systems, automation, and product engineering.

## Shorter intro

I’m a software engineer at Publix with 4 years of experience across automation, systems, analytics, workflow tooling, and operational software. I’m strongest where reliability, ownership, and practical engineering discipline matter.

## Best stories to prepare

### 1. Publix systems story

Tell this when asked:

- Tell me about your current role
- What kind of software do you work on?
- What’s a meaningful project you’ve owned?

Answer shape:

At Publix, I work on software that supports warehouse, pharmacy, and internal operations. That includes modernization work, analytics, workflow tooling, and QA automation. One of the strongest examples is helping modernize warehouse-control related systems and supporting legacy warehouse jobs and integrations. The work matters because these systems affect real operational flow, so I’ve had to think not just about code, but about reliability, regression risk, rollout confidence, and supportability.

### 2. QA automation story

Tell this when asked:

- Tell me about automation work you’ve done
- How have you improved testing?
- Why are you interested in SDET or QA automation roles?

Answer shape:

One area I’m proud of is leading QA automation for warehouse-control modernization work. I used Playwright, CI/CD pipelines, and Azure Key Vault to help make validation more repeatable and reliable. What I liked about that work is that it wasn’t just writing tests for the sake of coverage. It was about improving release confidence for software that had real downstream operational impact. That’s the kind of automation work I enjoy most.

### 3. Ownership / support story

Tell this when asked:

- Tell me about a high-pressure situation
- How do you handle production responsibility?
- Have you worked on regulated or critical systems?

Answer shape:

I’ve served as lead support engineer for controlled-substances software and file balance monitoring across 40+ warehouses. That kind of work teaches you to stay calm, be precise, and think clearly about impact. In those situations, it’s not enough to be clever. You need to understand the system, communicate well, and reduce risk quickly and responsibly.

### 4. Mentoring story

Tell this when asked:

- Have you led anyone?
- Do you mentor teammates?
- How do you work with junior engineers?

Answer shape:

I’m not presenting myself as a formal people manager, but I have taken on growing leadership and mentoring responsibilities. I’ve helped newer teammates with testing discipline, documentation, CI/CD habits, and practical use of AI-assisted delivery. Before Publix, I taught STEM courses, so helping people ramp up and understand things clearly comes naturally to me.

### 5. Side-project story

Tell this when asked:

- What have you built outside work?
- Are you product-oriented?
- How do you learn new systems?

Answer shape:

Outside of work, I build projects like Rally, a tennis lifestyle app, and GATEKPT, an AI-assisted workflow tool for music production. Those projects let me exercise different muscles than enterprise work, especially product thinking, interface design, state management, and end-to-end ownership. I like that combination because it keeps me sharp both as a builder and as an engineer.

## If they ask about AI helping with coding

Good answer:

Yes, I use AI as part of my workflow, mostly the same way I’d use documentation, examples, or a strong assistant when I’m exploring options faster. But I still own the work. I review the output carefully, adapt it to the system, debug it, and make sure I can explain the tradeoffs. For me, AI is a speed tool, not a replacement for engineering judgment.

## If they ask why you want to leave Publix

Good answer:

I’ve learned a lot there and I’ve had meaningful exposure to operational systems, automation, analytics, and support ownership. At this point I’m looking for a role with more room to grow, broader ownership, and a stronger path forward in software engineering.

Avoid:

- complaining
- saying they are stunting your growth
- sounding resentful

## Common questions and what they really want

### Why should we hire you?

They want signal that you can contribute fast and be trusted.

Good angle:

Because I’ve worked on software with real operational consequences. I know how to think about reliability, testing, support, and adoption, not just implementation. I can contribute as an engineer, and I also bring a calm, ownership-oriented mindset.

### What are your strengths?

Good angle:

- reliability mindset
- automation and testing discipline
- comfort with messy real-world systems
- communication and mentoring

### What’s a weakness?

Good angle:

Earlier in my career I could spend too long trying to make something perfect before sharing it. I’ve gotten better about working in smaller slices, sharing earlier, and using feedback to move faster without lowering quality.

## Technical prep focus

For software engineer interviews:

- explain one warehouse/system integration problem clearly
- explain one data/reporting flow clearly
- explain one side project architecture clearly
- practice array/string/hashmap problems at an interview-basic level

For QA automation / SDET interviews:

- know how to explain Playwright
- know what good test coverage means
- know how to talk about flaky tests
- know how CI/CD helps test reliability
- know how you would structure regression suites

## 7-day prep plan

### Day 1

- Practice your 60-second intro 10 times out loud
- Practice the “why leave Publix” answer

### Day 2

- Practice the Publix systems story
- Practice the QA automation story

### Day 3

- Practice the mentoring story
- Practice the side-project story

### Day 4

- Solve 3 easy coding questions
- Explain your answer out loud after each one

### Day 5

- Practice common behavioral questions
- Practice talking through one production/support scenario

### Day 6

- Review resume line by line
- Make sure you can explain every bullet honestly

### Day 7

- Do one full mock interview: intro, technical story, behavioral story, questions for them

## Questions to ask them

- What does success look like in the first 6 months?
- Is this role more weighted toward feature delivery, automation, support ownership, or systems improvement?
- How does the team handle quality, testing, and release confidence?
- What kind of ownership would I be expected to take on early?

## Final mindset

You do not need to sound like the smartest person in the room. You need to sound like someone they can trust with real software.
